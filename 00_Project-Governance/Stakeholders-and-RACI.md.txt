# Stakeholders and RACI

## Purpose

Cybersecurity is not solely the responsibility of the security team.

Security risks, incidents and remediation activities often require
coordination between technical teams, business owners, management,
privacy, legal and other organisational stakeholders.

This document identifies the primary stakeholders involved in the
MediCare Health Australia cybersecurity program and defines their
representative responsibilities.

---

# Key Stakeholders

## Board and Executive Management

Responsibilities:

- provide organisational oversight;
- understand significant cyber risks;
- approve strategic priorities and funding;
- review major cybersecurity risks and incidents;
- consider cybersecurity in business decision-making.

---

## Chief Information Security Officer / Security Manager

Responsibilities:

- lead the cybersecurity program;
- establish security strategy;
- oversee cyber risk management;
- approve security standards;
- coordinate major incident response;
- report significant cyber risks to management;
- oversee security improvement activities.

---

## Cybersecurity Analyst

Responsibilities:

- perform security assessments;
- analyse security risks;
- review security configurations;
- investigate alerts;
- support vulnerability management;
- perform control testing;
- collect technical evidence;
- support incident response;
- document findings and remediation.

For this project, the student primarily performs this role.

---

## Security Operations Centre

Responsibilities:

- monitor security events;
- triage alerts;
- investigate suspicious activity;
- escalate confirmed or high-risk events;
- maintain detection rules;
- support incident response.

The SOC function will be represented in the laboratory using
centralised logging, detection rules and controlled security events.

---

## IT Infrastructure Team

Responsibilities:

- manage servers and endpoints;
- manage Active Directory;
- implement approved configuration changes;
- deploy patches;
- maintain infrastructure;
- support backup and recovery;
- remediate technical findings.

---

## Clinical Systems Team

Responsibilities:

- manage clinical applications;
- understand clinical system dependencies;
- coordinate security changes affecting clinical services;
- assist with testing and remediation;
- protect system availability and patient-care workflows.

---

## Privacy Officer

Responsibilities:

- assess privacy implications of security incidents;
- coordinate privacy-related investigation;
- assess potential data-breach impacts;
- support regulatory notification assessment;
- maintain privacy governance.

---

## Human Resources

Responsibilities:

- notify relevant teams of employee lifecycle changes;
- support Joiner, Mover and Leaver processes;
- coordinate access changes associated with employment changes;
- support security awareness activities.

---

## Service Desk

Responsibilities:

- support user access requests;
- perform authorised password resets;
- escalate suspicious account activity;
- assist with endpoint support;
- follow identity-verification procedures.

---

## System and Data Owners

Responsibilities:

- determine business requirements;
- approve appropriate access;
- understand information sensitivity;
- participate in risk decisions;
- approve or reject relevant risk treatments;
- support periodic access reviews.

---

## Internal Audit / Independent Assurance

Responsibilities:

- independently assess governance and control effectiveness;
- review evidence;
- identify control weaknesses;
- report assurance findings.

This function will not be independently performed in this student
project.

Project testing will therefore be described as self-assessed control
assurance.

---

# RACI Model

RACI means:

- **R — Responsible:** performs the work.
- **A — Accountable:** ultimately owns the outcome.
- **C — Consulted:** provides input before or during the activity.
- **I — Informed:** receives relevant information about the activity.

# Project Role Limitation

Because this is a single-student project, the student will simulate
several enterprise cybersecurity responsibilities.

This does not mean that these responsibilities would normally belong
to one person in a real healthcare organisation.

Where practical, the project will separate:

1. control implementation;
2. control testing;
3. evidence collection;
4. finding documentation;
5. remediation; and
6. retesting.

This separation improves the reliability and transparency of the
project evidence even though independent assurance cannot be achieved.
