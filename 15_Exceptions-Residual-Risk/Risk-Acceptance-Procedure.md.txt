# Cybersecurity Risk Acceptance Procedure

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how residual cybersecurity risk may be
formally accepted.

Risk acceptance is a governance decision.

It does not mean:

- the risk disappeared;
- the vulnerability was fixed;
- cybersecurity approved the weakness;
- no further monitoring is required.

It means an authorised Risk Owner has consciously agreed to retain
the documented residual exposure for a defined period or circumstance.

---

# 2. Preconditions

Before material cybersecurity risk may be accepted:

1. the risk scenario must be documented;
2. affected assets and services must be identified;
3. inherent risk must be assessed;
4. relevant existing controls must be identified;
5. control effectiveness should be assessed where practical;
6. treatment alternatives must be considered;
7. compensating controls should be considered;
8. residual risk must be assessed;
9. cybersecurity advice must be documented;
10. the acceptance authority must have appropriate authority;
11. a review date must be established.

---

# 3. Required Acceptance Record

A material risk-acceptance record should contain:

- Risk ID;
- risk title;
- Risk Owner;
- affected service;
- inherent risk;
- controls relied upon;
- known control weaknesses;
- treatment performed;
- treatment not performed;
- reason further treatment is not currently pursued;
- residual likelihood;
- residual impact;
- residual rating;
- cybersecurity recommendation;
- business justification;
- acceptance authority;
- acceptance date;
- expiry/review date;
- monitoring requirements;
- conditions of acceptance.

---

# 4. Acceptance Authority

Representative project model:

| Residual Risk | Representative Authority |
|---|---|
| Low | Operational / System Owner |
| Medium | Business or Technology Risk Owner |
| High | Senior Risk Owner with CISO consultation |
| Critical | Executive-level formal review |

Risks involving material clinical consequences may require clinical
leadership consultation regardless of numerical score.

---

# 5. Time-Limited Acceptance

High and Critical residual risks should not be treated as permanently
accepted without reassessment.

Acceptance should define:

- review date;
- expiry where appropriate;
- monitoring requirements;
- conditions requiring early reassessment.

---

# 6. Early Reassessment Triggers

Accepted risk must be reconsidered where:

- active exploitation is identified;
- a security incident occurs;
- compensating controls fail;
- new vulnerabilities are identified;
- business usage changes;
- clinical dependency changes;
- vendor remediation becomes available;
- architecture materially changes;
- threat conditions increase.

---

# 7. Cybersecurity Disagreement

Cybersecurity may recommend against accepting a risk.

Where an authorised business Risk Owner accepts the risk despite that
recommendation:

- cybersecurity advice should remain documented;
- the accountable acceptance decision should be recorded;
- appropriate escalation should occur;
- monitoring requirements should be established.

The risk record should not be altered to imply that Cybersecurity
considered the exposure acceptable if it did not.

---

# 8. Acceptance Closure

Risk acceptance ends when:

- the acceptance expires;
- the risk is remediated;
- the system is decommissioned;
- circumstances materially change;
- the risk is reassessed under a new decision.
