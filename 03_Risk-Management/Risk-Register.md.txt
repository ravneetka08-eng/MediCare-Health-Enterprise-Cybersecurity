# 1. Purpose

This register records cybersecurity risks identified across the
fictional healthcare enterprise and provides traceability between:

- business services;
- threats;
- vulnerabilities and control weaknesses;
- enterprise assets;
- crown-jewel assets;
- information classifications;
- inherent risk;
- existing controls;
- control effectiveness;
- treatment actions;
- security exceptions;
- residual risk;
- risk ownership;
- control testing;
- supporting evidence.

This is a living risk register.

Risk ratings and control assessments may change as technical
implementation, control testing, vulnerability assessment and
incident exercises produce additional evidence.

`03_Risk-Management/Risk-Assessment-Methodology.md`

Risk Score:

Ratings:

- Low: 1–4

A numerical rating does not replace consideration of:

- clinical consequences;

## 4.2 Risk Scenario

There is a risk that an external threat actor obtains valid workforce
authentication credentials through credential theft, social
engineering, endpoint compromise, password reuse or another
credential-compromise technique and uses the compromised identity to
authenticate to organisational systems.

If the compromised identity has access to clinical, administrative or
technology resources, the attacker may perform activity using an
apparently legitimate workforce identity.

This could result in:

- unauthorised patient-information access;
- account impersonation;
- additional credential theft;
- lateral movement;
- unauthorised changes;
- privilege escalation;
- security-control bypass;
- persistence;
- operational disruption.

---

## 4.3 Threat Profile

| Attribute | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Primary Threat Actor | External cybercriminal |
| Secondary Threat Source | Compromised third party or malicious actor controlling a legitimate account |
| Initial Access Vector | Credential theft / social engineering / endpoint compromise |
| Required Access | Valid workforce credentials |
| Potential Progression | Authentication → resource access → discovery → lateral movement / data access |
| Primary Security Domain | Identity |
| Detection Dependency | Authentication, endpoint and centralised security telemetry |

---

## 4.4 Preconditions / Contributing Factors

The scenario becomes more likely or more damaging where one or more
of the following conditions exist:

- MFA is absent or insufficient;
- passwords are weak or reused;
- users are vulnerable to credential phishing;
- authentication events are not centrally monitored;
- privileged access is granted to standard accounts;
- excessive group memberships exist;
- dormant accounts remain active;
- endpoint compromise exposes credentials;
- remote access is insufficiently restricted;
- unusual authentication behaviour is not investigated;
- compromised credentials provide broad access to multiple services.

These conditions are contributing factors and are not assumed to be
present until verified.

## 4.7 Impact Assessment

### Clinical / Patient-Care Impact

**Rating: 4 — Major**

Rationale:

Compromise of an identity with access to clinical systems could
interfere with access to clinical information or enable unauthorised
activity within systems supporting healthcare delivery.

The project does not independently assert that credential compromise
would necessarily result in patient harm.

Clinical consequences would require assessment by appropriate
clinical stakeholders in a real organisation.

### Confidentiality Impact

**Rating: 5 — Severe**

Rationale:

A compromised authorised identity may enable unauthorised access to
Restricted patient or workforce information.

### Integrity Impact

**Rating: 4 — Major**

Rationale:

Depending on assigned permissions, a compromised account may enable
unauthorised modification of information or system configuration.

### Availability Impact

**Rating: 4 — Major**

Rationale:

Credential compromise may provide an initial access path for activity
that disrupts systems or contributes to a larger cybersecurity
incident.

### Privacy Impact

**Rating: 5 — Severe**

Rationale:

If the identity provides access to patient information, unauthorised
access or disclosure may create significant privacy consequences.

Actual notification or regulatory consequences would depend on the
specific facts of an incident and would require appropriate privacy
and legal assessment.

### Operational Impact

**Rating: 4 — Major**

Potential consequences include:

- account containment;
- password resets;
- access suspension;
- endpoint isolation;
- investigation;
- service disruption;
- broader identity review.

### Financial Impact

**Rating: 3 — Moderate**

Potential costs may arise from:

- incident response;
- recovery activity;
- specialist support;
- service disruption.

No monetary loss value is asserted within the student project.

### Reputational Impact

**Rating: 4 — Major**

Material compromise involving patient information could reduce trust
in the organisation.

### Legal / Regulatory Impact

**Rating: 4 — Major**

Potential obligations may arise depending on the information affected
and incident circumstances.

Specific legal conclusions are outside the scope of this project.

---

## 4.8 Overall Inherent Impact

**Impact: 5 — Severe**

The overall rating is driven primarily by credible confidentiality
and privacy consequences involving Restricted patient information and
the potential role of compromised identity in broader system
compromise.

---

## 4.9 Inherent Likelihood

**Likelihood: 4 — Likely**

### Rationale

Credential compromise is considered a credible threat scenario for a
large healthcare-style enterprise because:

- the environment contains many workforce identities;
- users require access to multiple digital services;
- identity provides a pathway to sensitive systems;
- social engineering and credential theft are realistic enterprise
  threat vectors;
- successful compromise may initially resemble legitimate user
  activity.

This rating represents the project's scenario assessment and does not
claim to measure the actual incident likelihood of a real healthcare
organisation.
