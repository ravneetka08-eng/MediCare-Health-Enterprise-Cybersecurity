# Cybersecurity Risk and Control Traceability Model

## Purpose

This document defines how business context, cybersecurity risk,
security controls, testing, evidence, findings and remediation are
linked throughout the project.

The objective is to maintain end-to-end traceability.

# 4. Evidence Rules

Evidence should demonstrate what was actually performed.

Evidence should not be created merely to populate a repository.

Where practical, evidence records should capture:

- Evidence ID;
- date/time;
- test performed;
- system;
- operator;
- expected result;
- actual result;
- screenshot/output/log reference;
- related Control ID;
- related Test ID;
- related Risk ID;
- result;
- limitations.

Secrets, credentials and sensitive configuration must not be committed
to the public repository.

---

# 5. Failed Controls

A failed test must not be hidden.

A failed test creates useful assurance information.

Example:

TEST-IAM-002

Expected:

Reception user cannot access restricted clinical directory.

Actual:

Access succeeds.

Result:

FAIL

        |
        v

F-001 — Excessive Reception Access

        |
        v

Risk:

R-002 / R-005

        |
        v

Treatment:

Remove inappropriate group membership.

        |
        v

Retest:

TEST-IAM-002-R1

Expected:

Access denied.

Actual:

Access denied.

Result:

PASS

This demonstrates:

- detection of a control weakness;
- risk assessment;
- remediation;
- validation.

---

# 6. Evidence Integrity

Evidence should preserve sufficient context to allow another person to
understand:

- what was tested;
- when it was tested;
- against which system;
- why the test existed;
- what the expected result was;
- what actually occurred.

Where technically appropriate, hashes may be retained for forensic or
evidence-integrity purposes.

Not every ordinary configuration screenshot requires forensic hashing.

Evidence controls should be proportionate to the purpose.

---

# 7. Traceability Maintenance

Traceability references must be updated when:

- new risks are created;
- controls are implemented;
- tests are performed;
- findings are raised;
- treatment actions are created;
- exceptions are approved;
- incidents identify control failures;
- residual risk changes.

Traceability should represent the current project state rather than
becoming static documentation.
